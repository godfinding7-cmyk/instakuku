Copyright basics

Downloading a Reel is not the same as owning it.

Updated September 20, 2026 · 7 min read

This article is general information, not legal advice. Copyright rules vary by jurisdiction and context.

Public does not mean public domain

A creator can make a Reel publicly viewable while still owning copyright in the video, audio, graphics or other creative elements. Public access usually means people can view the post under the platform's rules; it does not automatically grant everyone a license to republish it elsewhere.

Low-risk use cases

Downloading your own Reel, saving a client's Reel under an agreed workflow, or keeping media when the copyright owner has clearly authorized you to do so are straightforward examples of permission-based use.

Attribution is not a substitute for permission

Giving credit can be respectful and sometimes required by a license, but credit alone does not automatically create a right to copy or repost a work.

Music can have separate rights

A Reel may combine video owned by one person with music licensed through Instagram. Permission from the video creator may not automatically grant off-platform rights to the soundtrack.

Transformative or fair-use arguments are context-specific

Some legal systems have exceptions such as fair use or fair dealing, but they depend on facts including purpose, amount used and market impact. A downloader cannot determine that legal question for you.

Practical rule

If you did not create the Reel and no clear license applies, ask the rights holder before republishing it. Keep a record of the permission when the intended use is commercial.
